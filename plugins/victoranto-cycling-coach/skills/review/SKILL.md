# Review a period

Read `references/coaching-principles.md`, `references/athlete-profile.md`, `references/data-sources.md`, `references/zones-and-sessions.md` and `references/evidence.md`.

## 1. Period and data

- Default period: the last calendar month, or the month named ("/review October"). Also accept "last 6 weeks", "this block", "the year".
- Always compare with the previous period of the same length.
- Pull completed training, planned events, load/fitness/fatigue and wellness for both periods from the connected sources. Never double count rides that appear in two sources. If data is thin, say what is missing and review what exists.

## 2. What to analyze

1. **Volume and load:** hours, distance, elevation and load per week; fitness and fatigue trend (CTL/ATL/TSB or equivalent) vs the previous period. Load models are context, not verdicts.
2. **Compliance:** planned vs done, which sessions were skipped or changed, and any pattern (always the same weekday, always the hard session, always after long work days). If the period included time off the bike, judge it against the alternative plan, not against bike volume, and compare bike numbers with that in mind.
3. **Intensity distribution:** time per zone and whether it fits the phase (base, build, specific).
4. **Performance:** best 5, 20 and 60-min power vs the previous period; W/kg with current weight; whether the FTP needs re-evaluating.
5. **Durability:** on the long rides, power and HR in the final hour vs the early hours, and how that changed over the period. This matters most for ultra-distance goals.
6. **Other activities:** how much gym, swimming, running, hiking, and how they interacted with the bike sessions.
7. **Road to the A race:** weeks left; whether base and specific work are where they should be (for example long enough rides, enough gravel or climbing, fueling rehearsed); what is missing.
8. **Yearly distance goal** (only if the profile has one): distance so far this year (counting only the riding types the athlete chose, never double counting), % of the goal, distance left, weeks left, km per week needed vs the recent weekly average, and whether they are ahead, on track or behind. If behind, say what weekly increase would close the gap and whether that fits their recovery and plan.
9. **Recovery:** sleep, resting HR, HRV trends if available; deloads done vs needed.

## 3. Output

Reply in the profile's language and units.

- Short tables for the numbers (period vs previous period, with the change).
- **Verdict** in 2-3 lines: what is working, what is not, the main limiter.
- **Adjustments for the next period:** 2-4 concrete changes (for example "add a 5 h ride every two weeks", "bring the deload forward", "FTP test next Tuesday", "move gym legs away from Friday").
- Warnings that apply (FTP re-test, deload, fatigue pattern, fueling issues on long rides).

**Optional visual page:** offer, in one line, a page with charts (weekly load, time in zones, power curve vs previous period) that the athlete can open on their phone and keep. Build it only if they say yes.
