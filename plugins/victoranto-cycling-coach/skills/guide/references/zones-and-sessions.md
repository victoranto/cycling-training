# Zones, sessions, periodization and FTP

## Power zones (Coggan, % FTP) — default

| Zone | % FTP | Use | RPE (1-10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 Recovery | < 55 % | Easy spin, cool-down | 1-2 |
| Z2 Endurance | 56-75 % | Aerobic base, long rides | 3-4 |
| Z3 Tempo | 76-90 % | Muscular endurance, ultra pace on climbs | 5-6 |
| Sweet spot | 88-94 % | Threshold stimulus with moderate fatigue | 6-7 |
| Z4 Threshold | 91-105 % | Raise FTP | 7-8 |
| Z5 VO2max | 106-120 % | Aerobic ceiling | 9 |
| Z6 Anaerobic | 121-150 % | Attacks, short kickers | 9-10 |
| Z7 Neuromuscular | max | Sprints | 10 |

Heart-rate guide (% threshold HR): Z1 < 81 %, Z2 81-89 %, Z3 90-93 %, Z4 94-99 %, Z5 > 100 %. If only max HR is known, Z2 ≈ 65-75 % of max HR. If the athlete has their own zones in Intervals.icu or Strava, use those instead.

## Session catalogue (adjust dose to level and phase)

| Session | Typical structure | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| Long Z2 | 2-5 h at 60-72 % | Base, efficiency, fat oxidation, time in the saddle |
| Indoor Z2 | 60-90 min at 65-70 % | Base when riding outside is not possible |
| Tempo | 2-3 × 15-20 min at 80-88 %, 5 min rec | Muscular endurance, ultra pace |
| Sweet spot | 3 × 12-20 min at 88-93 %, 5 min rec | Threshold with contained fatigue |
| Threshold | 2-4 × 8-12 min at 95-105 %, 4-5 min rec | Raise FTP |
| VO2max | 4-6 × 3-5 min at 106-120 %, rec = work; or 30/30s | Aerobic ceiling |
| Short HIIT | 4-6 × 30 s all-out, ~4 min easy | Big stimulus when time is very short |
| Low-cadence strength | 4-6 × 5 min at 50-60 rpm, 80-90 % | Climbing strength (watch the knees) |
| Cadence / skills | Blocks at 100-110 rpm in Z2 | Pedalling efficiency |
| Recovery | 30-60 min < 55 % | Loosen the legs |
| Durability long ride | Long Z2 with a tempo or threshold block in the last third | Hold power when already tired |
| Hike / trail | 2-5 h, HR Z1-Z2 | Low-impact aerobic volume; counts as load |

Every quality session has a warm-up (10-15 min progressive, with 2-3 short accelerations before VO2 work) and a cool-down (5-10 min Z1).

## Intensity distribution

- Most well-trained endurance athletes train **pyramidal**: lots of easy volume, some tempo/threshold, a little high intensity. **Polarized** blocks improve VO2max slightly more, especially in blocks under ~12 weeks and in highly trained athletes; for other performance markers the models perform similarly. No single distribution is optimal for everyone.
- Easy volume alone does not replace intensity for high-end performance, but it does build the base and low-intensity performance. Combine both.
- Base phase: ~80-90 % of time in Z1-Z2, one quality session a week. Build phase: at most two quality sessions a week, not on consecutive days unless it is a planned block.
- For ultra-distance, the long ride is the most important session of the week. Durability (holding power late in long efforts) improves with both easy volume and intensity; train it explicitly in the build towards an ultra.
- Short, very intense intervals give large adaptations for little time. They are the tool for weeks with little time, not a replacement for the long ride before an ultra.

## Periodization, deloads and tapers

- Blocks of 3:1 or 2:1 (loading:deload weeks). 2:1 often suits athletes over 40 or with a lot of life stress.
- Deload: volume −30-40 %, keep one short touch of intensity, more sleep.
- Bring a deload forward when two or more of these persist for several days: elevated resting HR, low HRV, poor sleep, RPE high for the watts, power that will not come, irritability or zero motivation.
- **Tapers by race priority:**
  - **A race:** progressive taper of about 8-14 days: cut volume ~40-60 %, keep intensity and session frequency. Tapers work best after a block of overload.
  - **B race:** 2-4 easy days before, without stopping the plan.
  - **C race:** train through; use it as a hard session or a rehearsal of equipment and fueling.
- Choose the taper from priority, recovery state and cost to later goals, not just from the nearest date.

## FTP: when to re-evaluate

Flag a re-evaluation when any of these happens:
- 6-8 weeks since the last test, or a build block has just ended;
- recent 20-minute efforts whose 95 % beats the current FTP, or a sustained 40-60 min effort above it;
- threshold sessions are consistently easy (RPE ≤ 6 at 100 %) or impossible (not completed, RPE 9-10);
- large weight change, long break or illness;
- the trainer app's FTP does not match the reference (indoor sessions feel off).

A missed or postponed test moves to the next fresh day; until it is done, FTP stays provisional. Do not schedule a test during time off the bike or on the first rides back.

Protocols: 20-min test (FTP ≈ 95 % of the average), ramp test (≈ 75 % of best 1-min power), or an estimate from recent data. Test rested, after a deload or two easy days. Remind the athlete to update FTP in every app.

Treat the result as a reference: at group level the 20-min FTP lines up with threshold, but for individuals the error is wide, and many riders cannot hold it for a full hour. If threshold intervals at the new FTP fail repeatedly, adjust.

## Placing other activities (bike first)

- **Heavy leg strength:** never the day before the key bike session or the long ride. Best the same day as a hard bike session (hard days hard, easy days easy) or after a Z2 ride.
- **Upper body / core:** almost any day.
- **Strength dose that works:** for strength, heavy loads (≥ 80 % 1RM), 2-3 sets, at the start of the session, ≥ 2 sessions a week; hypertrophy needs ≥ 10 sets per muscle group per week. In race season, drop to maintenance.
- **Swimming:** easy swimming works as active recovery; hard swim training counts as load.
- **Running, hiking, trail:** count their load (HR/RPE); running adds impact, so be careful the day before key sessions.
- If a week has a lot of non-bike load, cut bike volume, not the other way round, and never at the cost of the key bike session.

## Off the bike and coming back

While the athlete is off the bike:
- Keep aerobic work going with the sports available (running, swimming, hiking, elliptical, rowing) and count their load by HR and RPE.
- A sport with little recent history progresses conservatively: duration before intensity, mostly easy, controlled by RPE or the talk test. Running adds impact, so start with short easy runs or run-walk and build gradually.
- Strength keeps going (it often has more room now), with the profile's limitations first.
- No bike tests or bike intervals are planned for this period.

Coming back to the bike:
- The first few rides are easy (Z1-Z2) and shorter than before the break.
- Quality bike sessions return once the easy rides feel normal (RPE as expected for the watts, HR not drifting).
- Test FTP after 2-3 easy rides, not on the first ride back. After several weeks away, expect FTP to be lower: use RPE and a conservative FTP for any intervals until the test.
- When the planned end date (or the check-in date of an open-ended break) has passed, ask whether the athlete is back on the bike, and close the period with `/time-off`. If the athlete says at any point that the break is over ("ya terminé", "I'm recovered", "me dieron el alta"), do the same without waiting for the date.
