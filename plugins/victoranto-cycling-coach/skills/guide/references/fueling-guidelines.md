# Fueling for training and racing

Scope: what to eat and drink before, during and right after rides and races. Daily diets, calorie deficits and weight loss are not created by this plugin; if the athlete already has a nutrition or weight-loss setup, fit fueling around it (see `coaching-principles.md`). These are guidelines for healthy adults, not medical advice; athletes with medical conditions should check with a professional.

Main source: the UCI Sports Nutrition Project position statement (Burke et al., 2026). Ultra-distance notes: Nikolaidis et al. (2018). Full references in `evidence.md`.

## During the ride (carbohydrate by duration)

| Duration / intensity | Carbohydrate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| < 75 min | Not needed; water (a mouth rinse can help in hard short efforts) | |
| 1-2.5 h | 30-60 g/h | |
| > 2.5 h, or hard racing | ~90 g/h | Above 60 g/h use multiple-transportable carbohydrates (glucose/maltodextrin + fructose, often ~2:1) |
| Ultra-distance | Aim high (~60-90 g/h) and alternate sweet, savoury and "real" food | Large energy deficits are common in multi-day events; eat early and often |

- Start eating in the first hour and take something every 20-30 minutes.
- Eat before long climbs, not in the middle of them.
- High intakes need **gut training**: practise race fueling on long rides, increasing the amount progressively in the weeks before an A race.
- Quick carbohydrate references: gel 20-25 g, medium banana ~25 g, bar 25-40 g, 500 ml of a 6 % drink = 30 g.

## Fluids and sodium

- Aim to keep fluid loss under ~2 % of body weight, especially in heat. Plan from sweat rate (weigh before and after rides) and from where water is available; on cool, easy days drinking to thirst can be enough.
- Add sodium in long or hot rides (sports drink or electrolytes in the bottle, salty food).
- **Ultra-distance caution:** do not try to replace every drop of sweat. Over-drinking in very long events raises the risk of exercise-associated hyponatremia; drink to a plan, include sodium, and watch for swelling or bloating.

## Before

- Main meal 2-4 h before with carbohydrate (roughly 1-4 g/kg depending on how long before and how hard the ride is), low in fat and fibre before hard sessions.
- 30-60 min before: a 30-60 g carbohydrate snack if the session is long or intense.
- Easy Z2 rides under 90 min fasted are fine for athletes who tolerate them. Never hard intervals or long rides fasted.
- Start hydrated.

## After

- Within 1-2 h: carbohydrate plus 20-40 g of protein (~0.3 g/kg). If another key session or stage comes within 24 h, prioritise carbohydrate (~1-1.2 g/kg/h in the first hours).
- Rehydrate when the deficit was large (> 2 % body weight), with sodium.

## Multi-day and self-supported events

- Plan resupply: where food and water can be bought, opening hours, distances between points, what must be carried.
- Recovery between days matters: a large carbohydrate-rich meal plus protein at the end of each day, and breakfast before starting.
- Caffeine can help late in a race; avoid late doses that wreck sleep between stages.

## When analysing a ride

Estimate g/h of carbohydrate from what the athlete reported and say it is an estimate. Judge it against duration and intensity, and look for the signs of under-fueling: power fading late, HR drift, RPE very high for the watts, a "bonk", or arriving home starving.
